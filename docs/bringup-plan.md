# Revision A bring-up and characterization

All physical results: **TBD — HARDWARE MEASUREMENT REQUIRED**. Simulation results are not acceptance measurements. Log board revision, fitted parts, gain shunt, instruments, ambient light, temperature, supply, calibration and acquisition settings with each result.

**Before powered bring-up:** qualify C2 to retain at least 0.47 µF effective capacitance at 3.3 V, including DC bias, tolerance, temperature and aging. Typical manufacturer curves support the part choice but do not guarantee each lot; obtain adequate manufacturer qualification or sample measurements. Characterize C6 effective capacitance and verify its reference settling/noise during controlled bring-up before accepting performance. Keep the locked values and packages; see the [footprint and capacitor review](../hardware/manufacturing/rev-a/footprint-review.md).

1. **Visual inspection, power off.** Check OPA2320 pin 1, TPS7A2033PDBVR orientation, BPW34 cathode, BAT54S orientation, solder bridges and flux residue. Verify J1 against the contract, not a DevKit header's physical order. Close exactly one of JP2/JP3/JP4 (10 kΩ/100 kΩ/1 MΩ); leave JP1 (ADC connection) open. Inspect both board faces and clean the sensitive input region.
2. **Resistance checks.** Check VBUS_5V–GND, 3V3A–GND and 3V3D–GND for persistent near-shorts, allowing capacitors to charge. Confirm the selected feedback path with power removed; semiconductor paths can affect readings. Check connector continuity and ensure 3V3A and 3V3D are not shorted together.
3. **Analog rail.** Use a current-limited 5 V source with JP1 open. Set an initial 20 mA limit for the analog board alone; the ESP32 requires a separate appropriate current budget. Expect approximately 6 mA analog quiescent current by calculation, not a pass/fail measurement. Stop for unexpected heating or persistent current limiting. Measure TP_3V3A; compare with the regulator tolerance and measured input voltage.
4. **Reference.** After at least 0.5 s, measure TP_VREF near 1.65 V and check ripple. Never add a large capacitor directly to the reference buffer output. Verify the divider midpoint only with a high-impedance instrument.
5. **Dark baseline.** Shield BPW34 completely and measure TP_TIA, TP_FILTER and TP_ADC. Expect near VREF with gain-dependent dark/offset shift. Record the actual baseline for each gain; do not substitute nominal 1.650 V in precision current calculations.
6. **Oscillation.** Use a short scope ground spring at TP_TIA and TP_FILTER; inspect at high bandwidth and multiple time scales, including startup and each gain. Do not probe SUM with an ordinary capacitive probe. Stop if there is sustained oscillation, unexpected ringing or clipping.
7. **Photodiode response.** Apply weak controlled LED illumination. Output must decrease with increasing light. Use a resistor/current-limited LED driver; do not drive a high-current LED directly from a GPIO. Document wavelength, source drive and fixed mechanical geometry. Optical intensity is uncalibrated unless independently characterized.
8. **Three ranges.** Power off before moving the one gain shunt. Repeat baseline and response for 10 kΩ, 100 kΩ and 1 MΩ. To measure transimpedance accurately, use a calibrated injected current or independently characterized optical current. Fit ΔV/ΔI and record uncertainty; comparing an uncalibrated LED setting does not establish absolute gain.
9. **Saturation and bandwidth.** Increase controlled input toward the 0.25 V operating floor, then modestly beyond it to characterize recovery. Keep ADC disconnected during initial overload tests. Sweep a small sinusoidal modulation through the useful band and past the system −3 dB point (~310 Hz simulated). Normalize against measured input amplitude; source/LED roll-off must not be attributed to the TIA. Record clipping, bandwidth and recovery separately from simulation.
10. **ADC connection and calibration.** With JP1 open, power both boards from the intended common source, confirm common ground, J1 pin 3=DevKit 3V3D, and inspect rail ramps. The BAT54S clamps do not isolate an unpowered ESP32. Never hot-plug; open JP1 for independent powering. For ADC-only calibration, keep **JP1 / JP_ADC OPEN** and inject a current-limited DC source at **J1 pin 5 (ADC_LINK), the isolated ESP32/GPIO34 side**, **not TP_ADC or the actively driven filter output**, to avoid fighting the filter output. Measure the injected voltage with a DMM at ≥5 points across 0.25–2.40 V; retain raw counts, reported mV and DMM mV. Load the correction table in Python. Power down, remove the source, then fit JP1. Out-of-window values are invalid, not photocurrent measurements.
11. **Wi-Fi comparison.** Compare dark analog-output spectra and ADC records with Wi-Fi off/on and representative streaming load, especially at 1 MΩ. Use external instrumentation for the analog noise floor; the ESP32 ADC may dominate by orders of magnitude. Record actual sample timing, dropped counts and network conditions. Check connection loss/reconnection without joining separate epochs.
12. **WebSocket streaming and CSV capture.** Connect the Python dashboard to `ws://<esp32-ip>:81/` and use its `--record` option to save a CSV session. Verify JSON v1 batches and equal-length `raw`, `mv` and `dt_us` arrays. Record timestamped sessions with raw counts, baseline calibrated mV, correction-table provenance, gain and device timestamps. Match firmware gain metadata to the physical shunt. Verify all three ranges and default 4 ksps. Characterize 1–8 ksps settings separately; low sample rates provide weaker alias rejection.
13. **FFT/noise.** Use a stationary, gap-free dark record for noise estimation and a separate known-tone record for FFT accuracy. Inspect timing before FFT; reject discontinuities instead of treating irregular samples as uniform. Report bandwidth, record length, window, detrending, PSD units, total/AC RMS and DC baseline. PSD containing a signal is not a dark-noise measurement. Retain data and scripts; never relabel mock-server output as measured.
